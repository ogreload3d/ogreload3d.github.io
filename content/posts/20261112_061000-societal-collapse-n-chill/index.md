## 4 AM youtube is much different than 4 PM youtube

I don't know why but when I use youtube normally,
I end up getting the same 5 music videos over and over again.
4 AM youtube is very different, as it just gives me
bangers and mind benders.
Just what I need to feed my sleep-deprived brain,
with my baby's here on my lap
lazily sucking his milk outta the bottle.

[Last time I introduced the philosphy youtuber who uses
Big Dick Philosophy words like extrapolation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_l7-aHyjnDg)
etc to talk about gacha games.
Big Dick Philosphy isn't your mama's Malcolm Gladwell or Andrew Tate,
we're talking about real heavy swingers
like Emmanuel Cunt* and Mick Fuckall**.
I did not enjoy that feeding session,
but it did teach me the difference
between a story and a narrative.

Story is a series of events, narrative is the feeling behind it.
When I tell you "A man came, took the baby out of the bed,
and left the room," I told you a story. YOU have a narrative for that:
what the fuck just happened? If you are watching too much
Investigative Discovery and True Crime, you think the baby's been kidnapped.
For me, it's a another 2 AM feeding session.

Some youtubers like to tell the story beat by beat
and make a 4 hour movie of whatever, interspersed with some
most braindead takes you have ever seen.
[Wendigoon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=02Oh_rnNJF8) is in my shitlist.

I kinda like Art Chad sometimes, while he is kind of a doomer,
he also gives some nice books to read,
and always ends up with a positive note.

[In his latest video about the societal collapse.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xd74EDSxJfw)

The story between the
squirrels being turned into Chicken McNuggets and France rioting is
kind of a stretch. As a biologist, those squirrels couldn't be set free
since they can carry diseases that would kill the native ecosystem.
If you are a wuss, of course you'd be like "oh noooo the squirrels!"
but the airport did the right thing here.

Anyway the main points made is that the managerial class thinks problems
can be solved with more rules and regulations.
As rules get piled up, complex systems get too complex,
nobody knows what to do, then the system collapses under its own weight.
Tale as old as time.

I'm sure you have witnessed it yourself at the workplace:
somebody fucked up at work and manager makes a procedure
to safeguard against it.
Then another rule here, and another checkbox to tick there.
Before you know it, you'll end up with a 50 checkbox template just to
release or do anything!

These "managers" approach the workplace as they approach the computer.
They see their employees as something to program,
and procedures introduced as a vehicle to prove that the work was done.

Obviously this sort of workplace would require the employees
to be like docile little drones and stick to the script whenever possible.
The more ambitious will be told that they need to have "more impact,"
and what that impact will be definied as "I know it when I see it."

I think that you can agree that this isn't a desirable workplace.
But the thing is: how can "managers" manage if not by through orders and
procedures? Can you even program a society?

[Apparently there was a huge Chilean attempt to control the economy
via computers in the 70s. An idea much ahead of its time,
and like all revolutionary ideas, this one ended with a coup d'etat of its own.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Cybersyn)

"Collapse" is a very dramatic term, but what is a "collapse?"
Let's go back to the France example: France is having a riot,
they are building barricades once again as is per tradition.
It's one of those "Jokes" I have that the French only go to the office
when they can go on strike or take their vacation cheques.

The thing is that you can have the best intentions in the world,
but everything comes down to is the person interacting with it accepting it or not.
May it be austerity, traffic or even common decency.

I remember there was this just ONE month in Estonia where we could
drink in the public like you can do in Japan or Germany.
The problem was that when the news came out, then the drunkards
 would think that they had the RIGHT to drink anywhere they wanted.
Didn't matter if it was a shopping mall or a library: they were in a public space
so they had the RIGHT to neck their 2 liter double bock!

These "people" reminded me more of cattle, that every single step of the
way you had to beat them and force them and leave them no other choice
but to do the right thing.
I wanna ask is: is that a sign of a healthy society?

One thing about Estonians is that they LOVE to be so helpless when
they get inconvenienced, but always know their rights
what people can tell them they can or can't do.
Like unless there is a law that will prohibit you to drink in a library,
then that means you not only have the right,
but you MUST drink at the library!

People think that a "societal collapse" is a lack of law and order,
the breakdown of all rules and procedure, and "simplification of the society".
But isn't this complete lack of individual responsibility
and spirit an even worse form of "collapse?"

I think that if we want to survive as a Mankind towards the 22nd century,
we must take back our own collective spirit, and not demand, but
create the society we desire to live in.
One hot topic in childrearing is that people want their kids to read books.
Do the parents show that reading is cool to their kids? Nope, too tired!
Do the parents read books themselves? Nope, they doom-scroll on the couch.
How about even discussing compulsory reading? Nah!
It's as if the child is a guest staying at their house, and
somehow the child must absorb this idea that reading is good for them.

I'm of course not placing all the onus on the individual, that
if there are repressive mechanisms placed to screw them over,
then they should pick up their bootstraps and lift themselves up.
But we must try, since freedom isn't given, it's gained.

This is also why I'm also looking into all this self-hosting and indie
internet and BBS technology and stuff. Mastodon etc, fediverse,
whatever else we got. The thing is that we will cut the cord from
the Big Tech, and let Facebook go the way of MySpace, Orkut and the rest of the ilk.

Because think about it: tech is supposed to serve the user, not the user serve the tech.
The users train the algos, the services get shittier,
their data gets sold on, and the beat keeps on.

This addiction to BAD tech is a symptom of a sick society, just like
we keep on using the shittier services out of "convenience",
people also keep on electing shittier politicians etc.
At some point there must be the awakening
and we must say: "no more!"

I dread the day Google shuts down, since all my passwords and emails are in there.
What about all the accounts and passwords I got stored in Google?

Surely I'll figure out how to pick up the phone and talk to a REAL person!
