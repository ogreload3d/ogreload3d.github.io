## Pages that have stayed the same

This question has been on my mind for a time ever since I discovered
myself this book called "Thinking with Type". I haven't gotten much
into it yet. But the whole point about typography is that it's not just
"making text look good." It can be a lot more than that. And naively,
I thought I was the "big dog in the house", doing all this back-end coding,
making things so freaking fast. But if the user experience is terrible,
then any good idea turns into a weapon against the human psyche.

The weapons that are being used against us is social media.
The Internet. He who controls the past controls the future. Where can
you read about history? Where have you read a funny little history fact?
Most likely Wikipedia's "This Day in History" is as far as people get.
The social media is just this endless feed of
"things that happened and people have posted." Notice I have said "people",
[since you only spend 7% of your time reading about your actual friends.](https://www.brainknowsbetter.com/p/the-algorithm-ruined-my-secret-instagram)

The thing is that all of these pages, Facebook, Twitter, Instagram,
they all look exactly as they did as when I started high school.
I have since graduated high school, go into university,
graduate that, go through conscription, emigrate from Estonia,
change 8 different jobs. And these pages are still all the same.
Only things that have changed is the content.

## Can design cause violence?

It's a very provocative question. [Facebook has famously been an integral
part to the Myanmar massacre](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/09/myanmar-facebooks-systems-promoted-violence-against-rohingya-meta-owes-reparations-new-report/)
and Twitter is now just psychic damage in 140/280/whatever letters.
Plus the non-stop stream of pornography! Boy howdy, I thought
these pages are for y'know vehicles for advertisement, but I guess
I can and will be sold Tide pods during my goon sesh.

I am most concerned about the complete statis of design actually.
I have seen a bunch of youtube videos about these "designers" whining
about how AI creates the most human un-friendly UI.
(User Interface - the thing you can read and press buttons on) It just apes
whatever is "expected", but not what is needed, and all these
pages end up looking kind of the same.

The other part is that when you are using Twitter, then there are
a bunch of "Twitter-alternatives"... that just look the exact
same fucking way: some shmuck's face in a circle, their user name,
their stupid little post with a picture on the bottom. Scroll down
to see another dumbass face with their little text.

When you see the UI of Twitter and the UI of Mastodon, it will
invite you to post whatever you want. But **WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY POST?**
What is the expected thing? What will get you those "likes" or whatever.
Why the hell would you even post on such a horrible site for all these
strangers? Because you are all alone on Mastodon. You are the algo,
you are the bird who needs to find your feed.

Notice it's called content in a feed.
You know who else has content in their feed?

**PIGS!** Slop in a trough!

Hell, even this BLOG has a very convenient layout, so you wouldn't
freak out about it, so it would tell you that yes, this is a blog,
you can scroll down and see more and more.... You know you can expect
some dude writing crap like I'm Carrie from the Sex and the City.

But if you see the Twitter UI, and you see Mastodon UI,
does it even make you excited for what kind of content will you see?
You already come in with expectations what you can get, and you are all
set for doomscrolling with a little chuckle and an outrage guaranteed.

If the UI already tells you what you can expect, then it will
most definitely tell you what you can or cannot post. And if your
behavior has been controlled, your thoughts will be controlled
just the same.

**ACTIONS ARE THOUGHTS, PEOPLE!** That's why the actors are
so concerned not about what you think on the stage, but what you do.
[Read more about here, Acting is Action](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/acting-action-56935/),
or read more about the [Stanislavski's system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislavski%27s_system).
Get out of your mind and into your life.

So yes, to answer the question if web design can cause violence:
absolutely yes. 100% definitely.

## I'm not a doomer so a little fun to cleanse the palate

IDK man like ever since I have started spending time with my son, I have
dreaded that my freakin' work will start again in 10 days.
And y'know, it is what it is. I need to make money. I will make money.
I'm not sure if all the projects I left half-way done are getting done
or not, but that ain't my problem today.

I was so worried that I can't get my BBS up and running by the end of my
paternity leave. WHO CARES?! I do, but all good work takes time.
And I guess being with my son and wife is also important.

Work isn't like a rabbit that runs away - that's a great Estonian proverb
that I live by.

Anyway, I really think this paternity leave gave me a whole lot of time
to think about what I actually want, and would need to focus.
I think the next battlefield will not be about "code", but about UI/UX.
Any dumbass can layer a shit ton of features on top of eachother,
let them burn that money. Look at all these features that some shmuck "built!"

But if the average user is just turned off by your product, then guess what,
DEAD IN THE WATER! 0 sales, 0 nothing.


This is like another reason why I don't let AI anywhere near my
text or even any content I let the person see. AI does not know
how to give that je-ne-sais-quoi that only I can give.
