The one idea I did get was that the button press becomes the action, and the observing you becomes the character. Like playing Sekiro, you can do a shit-ton of things with the controller, but all you are doing is just mashing the buttons. You aren't jumping in the valley getting chased by the snake or whatever, you, the player, are on the couch pressing buttons. But you, the person observing, think that you are now the dude on the screen, doing all those things.

It's what the Japanese call 人馬一体 , *jinba-ittai*, typically meaning the man and horse become one, but can also mean that you and whatever become one. Not in a sexual sense of course, but more in a way that your conciousness melds together with whatever outside your body. This word was also used during motorcycle classes, and it was a very important concept to get it. You could not ride a motorcycle without melding together with the motorcycle. You could not just operate the motorcycle like a car, where you just push and pull levers and spin the steering wheel. You had to FEEL the motorcycle, where to look when turning, when to lean to take the curve, when to accelerate and when to change gears. If you did not FEEL the motorcycle, you would fall over. Simple as that.

The fact the human conciousness can do that is exploited by companies trying to make a quick buck out of you. Since you can feel things that are not happening to you, imagine things that are not real, then with some fancy effects they'll trap your attention and make you feel like you did something more substantial than staring at a LCD screen and holding your thumb against it. 

The point I'm trying to say is that be wary of where you put your feelings into. It's so easy to become misled, but it's also easy to get back on track when you pay attention on what's really going on.
